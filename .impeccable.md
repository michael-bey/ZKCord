## Design Context

### Users
- **Discord server admins** deciding whether to add ZKCord. They need to understand in seconds what it does (roles by passport age and nationality) and trust that members' documents stay private.
- **Demo audiences** (judges, investors, hackathon crowds) watching a live presentation. The page has to make the idea land in about 10 seconds from across a room.
- Members mostly arrive through the private verify link, not the homepage. The verify flow should stay calm and clear.

### Brand Personality
- Three words: **bold, confident, trustworthy**.
- Voice: plain and direct. It states what happens; it doesn't hype. No "most advanced", no "revolutionary".
- Emotional goal: "oh, that's clever" followed by "I trust this".

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Bold and graphic.** Poster-like: very large type, strong color blocks, high contrast. Visual presence over document-style pages.
- The hero must make the core idea visible: the passport goes in, only "18+" and the nationality come out.
- Subject vernacular to draw on: passports (MRZ lines, data page, visa stamps, chip symbol) and Discord (roles, role colors, member list).
- Theme: follows the system setting. Design light and dark as deliberate equals.
- **Anti-references:** the old neon purple padlock and glassmorphism look; a plain docs/gov-form page with no visual presence; SaaS card grids; purple-to-blue gradients.

### Design Principles
1. **Show, don't tell.** The core idea should be visible in the hero, not explained in paragraphs.
2. **One loud thing per screen.** Each section gets one dominant element; everything else stays quiet.
3. **Privacy is the product.** Visual metaphors should reinforce that data is withheld (redaction, stamps), not "secure tech" clichés (locks, shields, circuits).
4. **Readable from across a room.** Hero and key claims should work at demo distance.
5. **Plain words.** Sentence case, concrete verbs, no marketing filler.
